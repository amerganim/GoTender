# RTI request to BPPA — draft

Draft for you to review, complete and file **in your own name**. I have not
sent anything and cannot: this is a legal filing that has to come from you.

A Bangla version is at [rti-request-bn.md](rti-request-bn.md), and that is the
one to file — Form 'ka' and official correspondence are in Bangla. This English
version carries the reasoning behind each question.

Verify the procedural details in the box below against the current rules before
filing — the Act's fee schedule and response deadlines are the sort of thing
that gets amended, and I would rather you check than take my word for it.

> **Procedural points to confirm**
> - The Right to Information Act 2009 prescribes a standard application form
>   (Form 'Ka' / ফরম 'ক'). Ask BPPA's Information Officer for the current form
>   or download it from the Information Commission's site.
> - Confirm the current application fee and the per-page charge.
> - Confirm the response deadline and the appeal window and authority.
> - Under the Act you are **not required to state why** you want the
>   information. The framing below explains the purpose anyway, on the
>   judgement that a cooperative request gets a more useful answer than a bare
>   one. Remove it if you would rather not.

## Tone: cooperative, not adversarial

The aim is not to extract a grudging disclosure. It is to find out whether BPPA
will sanction what we are doing, and ideally to end up with an arrangement
rather than a tolerated practice.

An official data-sharing arrangement, if BPPA will grant one, is worth far more
than the crawler: it is a moat no competitor scraping the site would have. So
the request is written to make saying yes easy.

## Draft request

**To:** Designated Officer (Right to Information)
Bangladesh Public Procurement Authority (BPPA)
IMED, Ministry of Planning
Sher-e-Bangla Nagar, Dhaka

**Subject:** Request for information regarding access to, and permitted reuse
of, publicly published tender notice data on the National e-GP Portal

Respected Sir/Madam,

I am building a service that helps Bangladeshi contractors and suppliers — in
particular small and medium enterprises — find the publicly advertised tender
notices that are relevant to their line of business, so that they learn of
opportunities in time to prepare a responsive tender.

The information concerned is already published openly by BPPA on
eprocure.gov.bd and is viewable without a login. My intention is to work in a
manner BPPA sanctions, and I am therefore asking for guidance before, rather
than after, making any service publicly available.

I respectfully request the following information:

**1.** Whether BPPA provides, or is willing to provide, any official means of
obtaining publicly published tender notice data in bulk or machine-readable
form — for example an API, a data feed, a periodic export, or a data-sharing
agreement — and if so, the procedure and any conditions or fees that apply.

**2.** Whether BPPA has published, or will state, any policy governing
**automated access** to the public pages of the e-GP portal: in particular
whether automated retrieval of publicly available tender notices is permitted,
and any technical limits BPPA expects to be observed, such as an acceptable
request rate or times of day to avoid.

**3.** BPPA's position on **reuse and redistribution** of the factual contents
of published tender notices — such as tender ID, reference number, procuring
entity, district, procurement method, publication date and closing date — by a
third party providing a notification or search service to prospective
tenderers. I note the copyright statement on the portal and am seeking BPPA's
position on how it applies to this factual information.

**4.** Whether BPPA has at any time granted any third party permission,
licence, or a data-sharing arrangement for access to tender notice data, and if
so, the general terms on which such access is granted and how an applicant may
apply for the same.

**5.** The name and contact details of the officer or department at BPPA with
whom questions about data access and reuse should be raised on an ongoing
basis.

For completeness, the manner in which I currently access the portal is as
follows, and I would welcome correction if any part of it is not acceptable to
BPPA:

- Only pages that are publicly available without a login are accessed.
- Requests are sent one at a time with a minimum one-second interval, totalling
  roughly forty requests per half hour.
- The software identifies itself in every request with the project name and a
  monitored email address, so that BPPA can contact me directly.
- Tender documents hosted by BPPA are **linked to at source and are not copied
  or re-hosted**.
- Every page displaying this information states that it originates from the
  e-GP portal and advises users to verify against the original notice.

If any of the above is contrary to BPPA's expectations, I would be grateful to
be told, and I will change it.

I would be glad to meet or correspond further if that would be helpful.

Yours faithfully,

[Full name]
[Address]
[Mobile] · [Email]
[National ID number, if the form requires it]
[Date]

**Preferred mode of receiving information:** email to [your address], or
printed copy if BPPA prefers.

## Notes on the questions

**Question 4 may be refused or answered only in general terms.** Asking what
other parties have been granted can touch third-party commercial information,
which the Act treats differently. It is worth asking — the answer bears
directly on how the incumbents operate — but do not let it jeopardise the
rest. It is placed fourth deliberately, so a partial refusal leaves questions
1–3 answered.

**Question 3 is the one that matters most.** It is the open question from the
legal review that decides what may be stored and re-displayed. If BPPA answers
only one question, this is the one to press on appeal.

**Describing the crawler is a deliberate choice.** It is disclosure before
being asked, it is accurate, and it invites correction rather than objection.
If BPPA has a concern, far better to hear it now than after a public launch.

**Whatever comes back, record it.** A written position from the data owner —
permissive, restrictive or silent — is worth more than inference, and is what a
lawyer will want to see. File the reply alongside this document.

## After filing

- If no reply arrives within the statutory period, the Act provides an appeal
  to the Appellate Authority, and beyond that to the Information Commission.
  Both are ordinary steps, not escalation.
- Take the reply, plus [legal-review.md](legal-review.md), to a Bangladeshi
  lawyer with IP and IT experience. The reply narrows what they have to
  opine on, which makes their time cheaper.
- Until a reply is in hand, §8's warning is unchanged: the crawler is polite,
  identifiable and rate-limited, but that is engineering, not legal cover.
